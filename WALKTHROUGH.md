# Complete Moon and Mars walkthrough

## Flight to either world

1. Let the opening story finish or choose Skip. Select the destination and
   **Outpost mission**. The briefing distinguishes departure-date centre
   separation from the calculated illustrative flight path.
2. Board Odyssey. The cadet crosses the gantry. In the cockpit, click the amber
   LAUNCH switch or press E. The glove presses it and the countdown begins.
3. Watch ascent. Use F or the cabin button to change view. During cruise,
   choose **Isolate auxiliary bus** to keep fuel; downlink falls to 50%.
   Backup retains downlink but costs 3% flight fuel and three emergency units
   after landing. Suitable clips have Skip; the decision cannot be skipped.
4. On final approach, drag to orbit the craft through 360°, or press **F / Pilot view**
   for the cockpit. Descent uses the destination terrain and the live altitude.
   Press Space while velocity is **1–3 m/s**. Press Space
   again while attitude is **−5° to +5°**. Holding the key cannot lock both.
   An unsafe solution repeats only the final landing exercise.
5. After touchdown, the feet rest on the local terrain. The parked lander remains
   visible and blocks walking through its footprint. Step onto the surface and **Begin outpost mission**.

Use Go to objective for a terrain route, or walk with WASD. Drag to look around.
E works only beside the actual target. Show objective aims at it; F gives an
eye-level view. Dialogs pause play while you operate equipment and review choices.

## Moon: Haven — the first watch

| Stage | Practical action | One successful operating plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Distribution bus | E at Haven. Rotate tiles to connect the labelled input/output; Apply repair. | Protect life support; dim the greenhouse |
| 2. Habitat leak | E at Haven; inspect seals, filter and bus readings. | Fit a replacement gasket |
| 3. Radiation alert | Inspect shelter and the four-person crew manifest. | Move supplies into passive shelter walls |
| 4. EVA release | Check suit, seal inner hatch, vent, then open outer hatch, in that order. | Take the light science kit |
| 5. Field relay | Travel to the gold relay marker. E; reconnect its differently oriented circuit. | Fit a tested spare board |
| 6. Basalt sample | Go to science. E or Q; scan coordinates/elevation, collect, tag and seal. | Tag and seal one representative sample |
| 7. Return | Go back to Haven. E; stow the sample and close the airlock. | Turn off extra loads; replenish the suit |

The airlock door and pressure indicator respond to your sequence. The sample
moves from the ground to a canister and then the laboratory. Relay restoration
changes its lamp. Choice previews and the resource panel show the results.
The final plan transfers 30 nominal oxygen minutes and 20 Wh from Haven to
refresh the suit. The debrief lists all seven decisions and a four-hour forecast.

Success requires sufficient crew/food/water/power/health/habitat margin, restored
relay and at least 45 science points. A crew can survive yet fail the science
objective. Poor power, leakage or reserve decisions can abort the attempt.

## Mars: Jezero — hold the line

Follow the same launch and landing controls, with Mars selected. The landing
site, sky, gravity, dust, terrain coordinates and saved mission are different.
Use the same successful plans in stages 1, 2 and 4–7 above. At **stage 3**, inspect
the dusty equipment and choose **Brush the panels and protect the air filter**.
This consumes a kit, reduces dust attenuation and protects habitat condition.
Collect a documented Jezero sample at stage 6; the success threshold is 55 science.

The expanded instrument pack offers extra science but raises fatigue and oxygen
demand and slows walking. Powered shielding reduces dose while drawing energy.
Extended surveys consume additional time and suit energy. You can experience
these trade-offs by choosing them and watching the EVA map change.

## ASTRA, exploration and retries

Open ASTRA from the suit or cockpit. Try “Can I safely complete this EVA?”,
“Why did our power decrease?” and “Can the crew survive until resupply?”
Responses cite actual reserves, loads, time estimates and forecast assumptions.
Forecasting itself consumes no resources and does not change the clock.

Sky guide pauses the mission. Set a new UTC, rate, exposure or observer, aim at
an observable body, or use the telescope. Enhanced night is explicitly an aid.
The lunar far-side option is sky-only and hides Earth behind the Moon. Close
Sky guide to restore the original mission time. Solar system distinguishes
its exaggerated overview from physically sized scientific probe views.

Open **Haven systems** in the right HUD to view crew resources, request ASTRA
forecasts, save manually, or board an orbital practice flight. On narrow screens,
first open the HUD with **Mission** in the top bar.

After each decision, the next stage is checkpointed. Save current checkpoint
also records a later position and resources. On failure, Replay checkpoint
restores the latest checkpoint; Restart begins a new attempt. Saved Moon/Mars
checkpoints remain separate. Resume is available at destination selection.

At Haven, **Board Odyssey for orbital practice** starts a local Moon/Mars launch
and controlled return. It costs five emergency units; crew resources continue
consuming during its gravity-derived orbital period. This optional exercise can
reduce the resupply margin, so consult ASTRA first. It returns to your existing
outpost state. Choose another Earth journey from the debrief to visit the other
world, or enter Flight school for ten practical lessons per destination.
